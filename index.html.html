01
Design Identity

My Emerging
Design Identity

When I started this degree, my understanding of game design was fairly narrow - I assumed it was mostly about artistic execution and coding. That framing felt comfortable because it was concrete. What I've found this semester is that design is fundamentally about communication and intention. Every decision - a sound cue, a tutorial prompt, a UI element - shapes what someone feels and understands in that moment.

That shifted for me through Game Studies, where I looked at how The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild manages to guide players without ever stopping the experience to explain itself (Nintendo EPD, 2017). The game trusts its players. Every mechanic gets introduced through environment and consequence rather than pop-up instructions. Studying that made me realise I was engaging with interaction design inside a game context - and once I saw that, I couldn't unsee it.

What I'm most curious about now sits at that overlap between player experience and interaction design: how a designed system shapes the way someone navigates, feels, and makes decisions. My thinking has moved from "what do I want to make?" toward "what do I want people to experience?" - which feels like the more honest question at this stage of the degree.

At the start of Assessment 1, I knew games interested me but couldn't say much more than that. Now I can start to name why - it has something to do with how a well-designed game respects the person inside it. That's a design value I want to carry through the degree regardless of where I end up specialising.

03
Discipline Direction

Where I'm
Pointing

I enrolled in the Bachelor of Design (Game Development) knowing I was interested in games, but a semester in, I have a much broader sense of what game development actually involves. The thing that surprised me most is how genuinely collaborative it is. Good games rely on programming, visual communication, interaction design, audio, narrative, and psychology all working at once - it is not one person doing one thing.

I am still working out which area I want to go deeper in, but I have found myself consistently pulled toward questions about player experience and how interaction design sits within that. Through both Game Studies and Introduction to Interaction Design, I kept coming back to the same kind of problem: how do players learn a system intuitively? How does feedback tell someone what to do without spelling it out?

Researching adaptive audio in Breath of the Wild for Assessment 2 shifted how I think about sound in games (Nintendo EPD, 2017). I went in treating audio as atmosphere - background texture. What I found was that sound in that game is doing real design work: guiding attention, reinforcing mechanics, signalling danger, shaping emotion. That made me realise sound design sits much closer to interaction design and player experience than I had assumed.

04
Study Pathway

My Academic
Pathway

My pathway planning this semester has been about building across multiple areas before narrowing down later. The recommended first-year structure for the Bachelor of Design (Game Development) has already made it clear how connected the core units are - game studies, interaction design, visual communication, and professional practice are not really separate things (University of Canberra, 2026).

Game Studies helped me think analytically about how mechanics, systems, and player psychology shape an experience. Introduction to Interaction Design gave me practical prototyping skills through Figma and HTML/CSS. Visual Representation Techniques pushed my understanding of composition and Adobe workflows further than I expected in a first semester. And Professional Orientation - this unit - has been the one that made me stop and think more deliberately about where everything is heading.

Moving into the rest of the degree, I am drawn to units that build on what I have already started exploring: more in-depth interaction design, sound and audio within games, and anything that gets into creative technology and production pipelines. Based on feedback received this semester, I am planning to pick up Sound Design (12219) as my elective next semester, which aligns directly with the interest in adaptive audio I developed through Assessment 2. I do not feel ready to commit to one specialisation yet, and I would rather go into second and third year with a broader base than a narrower focus. The first semester has made me more aware of what I want to go deeper in - and there is still a lot I have not touched yet.

Right now I am less concerned with locking in a specific job title and more focused on building something useful across creative and technical areas. The things that pull at me most are player experience design, interaction design for games and interactive media, and working in environments where different disciplines have to talk to each other to get something made.

What draws me to these areas is the combination of analytical and creative problem-solving. Designing interactive experiences means understanding how someone interprets a system, responds to feedback, and emotionally connects with what they are inside. That kind of design thinking - grounded in the person experiencing it - is what I have found most interesting this semester.

Sound has also become something I want to keep exploring. Looking at adaptive audio in Breath of the Wild for Assessment 2 changed how I hear games (Nintendo EPD, 2017). Sound was doing things I had not noticed before - directing attention, confirming decisions, building tension, supporting accessibility - none of which felt like decoration once I understood it. That has made me want to look more into audio as a design discipline, even if it stays adjacent to my main focus rather than becoming the centre of it.

In games, that covers accessibility, representation, and building systems that respect rather than exploit the person playing.

Over the rest of the degree, I want to keep building through collaborative projects, game jams, and portfolio work - and ideally through Work Integrated Learning when that becomes available. Staying adaptable feels more valuable to me right now than narrowing too early.
